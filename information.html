Understanding Electrical Circuits

Discover the fundamental principles behind electrical circuits and how they work.

V

Voltage

Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points in a circuit. It represents the energy transferred per unit of charge.

Symbol: V

SI Unit: Volt (V)

Formula: V = I × R

I

Current

Electric current is the rate at which electrical charge flows through a conductor.

Symbol: I

SI Unit: Ampere (A)

Formula: I = V / R

Ω

Resistance

Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric current through a material.

Symbol: R

SI Unit: Ohm (Ω)

Formula: R = V / I

What is an electrical circuit?

An electrical circuit is a closed path that allows electric current to flow. A basic circuit typically contains a power source, conducting wires and an electrical component.

Basic components

  • Power source: provides electrical energy.
  • Conductors: provide a path for current.
  • Resistors: oppose the flow of current.
  • Switches: open or close a circuit.

What is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's Law describes the relationship between voltage, current and resistance in an ohmic conductor under constant physical conditions.

V = I × R

Relationship between the variables

  • At constant resistance, increasing voltage increases current.
  • At constant voltage, increasing resistance decreases current.
  • At constant current, increasing resistance increases the required voltage.

Example

A resistor has a resistance of 5 Ω and a current of 2 A flowing through it. Calculate the voltage.

Step 1: V = I × R

Step 2: V = 2 × 5

Step 3: V = 10 V

Series

Series Circuits

In a series circuit, components are connected along a single path.

  • Current is the same through each component.
  • Voltage drops add together.
  • Individual resistances add together.

Rₜ = R₁ + R₂ + R₃

Parallel

Parallel Circuits

In a parallel circuit, components are connected across multiple branches.

  • Voltage is the same across each branch.
  • Current divides between branches.
  • Equivalent resistance is lower than the smallest positive branch resistance.

1/Rₜ = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
